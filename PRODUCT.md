# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Italian-speaking guests in Northern Italy (and nearby Swiss/Ticino) who want to host an exceptional dinner at home without the work of cooking it. They are typically planning an occasion: an intimate anniversary for two, a small celebration, a special evening with friends (2–8 guests). They arrive curious and a little aspirational, weighing whether a private-chef evening is "for them." Their state of mind is exploratory and emotional, not transactional. A separate, much smaller audience is Luca himself, who uses the admin dashboard to manage menu, reviews, and images.

## Product Purpose

Lucas Table is the brand site for a private-chef service: Luca comes to the guest's home, turns their kitchen into a fine-dining restaurant for an evening, and lets them be a guest at their own table. The site exists to communicate the feeling of that experience and to convert interest into a personal conversation with Luca. Success is a qualified inquiry: a guest who reaches out with their date, guest count, and occasion in hand, ready to plan. The current site ends in raw contact links (email / WhatsApp / phone) with no structured path; the strategic gap is a conversion flow that feels like the start of being hosted, not a checkout.

## Brand Personality

Intimate luxury, warm. Three words: refined, personal, generous. The voice is Italian and emotional, written in full evocative sentences (the existing copy reaches for feeling: "il lusso di essere ospiti a casa propria"). It should feel like being welcomed by a friend who happens to be an extraordinary chef, quiet elegance over flash, hospitality over salesmanship. Confidence without coldness; exclusivity without snobbery.

## Anti-references

- Generic food-delivery apps (UberEats / Deliveroo): no cart-and-card SaaS energy, no transactional checkout feel. This is a bespoke experience, not an order.
- Cheap template restaurant sites (Wix trattoria clichés): no checkered tablecloths, stock-photo kitchens, or fake 5-star badges.
- Cold corporate SaaS: no sterile gridded navy-and-gray dashboard aesthetics on the public surface.
- Loud / discount-driven UX: no promo banners, urgency timers, or "book now and save" hard-sell.

## Design Principles

- **Hospitality over conversion mechanics.** Every interaction should feel like the beginning of being hosted. Lead with warmth and the experience; let the practical details (date, guests) follow naturally.
- **Let the experience speak.** Show the evening (imagery, named experiences, real menus, real reviews) rather than asserting "luxury" in adjectives.
- **Quiet confidence.** Restraint signals quality here. Generous space, considered typography, and a committed single identity color do more than effects or badges.
- **Italian voice, full sentences.** Copy is emotional and complete, not clipped marketing slogans. Respect the existing bilingual-but-Italian-first register.
- **One continuous evening.** The public site reads as a single scroll-driven atmosphere (one deep-green world, full-bleed imagery), not a stack of disconnected card sections.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA. The deep-green (`#0d3b2e`) surface with light text demands verified contrast (≥4.5:1 for body, ≥3:1 for large text); avoid washed-out gray-on-green. Form fields need visible labels, focus states, and error messaging that works without color alone. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` for any scroll or reveal motion. Content is primarily Italian; keep language attributes correct and copy legible at the serif/italic sizes used.
